Whole-House Renovation Timeline: How Long Each Stage Really Takes

A whole-house renovation is a marathon run in stages, and the timeline is where most projects go wrong. Homeowners plan for the work and forget the waiting: permits, inspections, material lead times, and the simple fact that trades cannot all be in the same room at once. A realistic timeline protects your budget as much as your patience.

Start with the money, because money sets the pace. Renovation Guide HQ's timeline planning guide frames whole-house work in three tiers: about $10,000 for a focused single-room refresh, about $25,000 for a multi-room update, and about $60,000 for a serious whole-house scope. Single rooms on their own typically run $10,000 to $50,000 depending on the room and the finishes. Knowing your tier before you call a contractor stops the most common timeline failure: starting work you cannot afford to finish.

The order of work is mostly fixed, and skipping ahead costs weeks. Demolition and structural work come first, then rough plumbing, electrical, and HVAC, then insulation and drywall, then flooring and cabinets, then fixtures and paint. Drywall alone sets the pace for a room: one room usually takes 5 to 10 days from hang to paint, and a whole house takes 1 to 3 weeks. Rushing drywall is the classic false economy — mud that is not dry cracks, and cracked mud means rework after you have already moved in.

Permits and inspections are the hidden calendar. A permit application can sit for days or weeks depending on your city, and each inspection is a gate: fail one and the next trade waits. Build inspection days into the schedule as real days, not as assumptions. The same goes for materials: custom cabinets, windows, and specialty tile can take weeks to arrive, so order them before demolition starts, not after.

The most honest advice is to plan backwards. Pick your move-in date, subtract the work, subtract the inspections, subtract the lead times — and if the start date that leaves you with is in the past, move the move-in date, not the quality. A free renovation cost calculator and the renovation timeline planning guide give you the numbers and the stage-by-stage sequence to build a schedule that survives contact with reality.
